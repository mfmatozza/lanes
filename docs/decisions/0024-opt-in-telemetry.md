# ADR-0024: Usage telemetry is opt-in, off by default, and minimal

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Deciders:** @mfmatozza
- **Related:** Phase 4 plan · Success metrics · [Security model](../architecture/security.md) · Privacy policy · ADR-0012 · issue #171

## Context
- The product promise is that code and prompts never leave the team's machines. The privacy policy says the self-hosted edition "sends **nothing** to us", and [security.md](../architecture/security.md) says: "Any future telemetry is **opt-in** and covered by an ADR."
- The success metrics name two sources that need data from us: "onboarding telemetry (opt-in)" for install time and a "server heartbeat" for weekly active teams. Neither exists.
- The beta has 10 design-partner teams. For 10 teams, most metrics can be read from the `lanes report` summaries and written feedback they choose to send (no calls: ADR-0012, ADR-0027). For Stage 2 (100 teams, no hand-holding) they cannot.
- A telemetry endpoint costs infrastructure and legal surface (consent, data processing, a retention policy), and ADR-0012 keeps pre-revenue spend minimal.

## Decision
Proposed: **opt-in telemetry, off by default, in two steps.**

**Step 1, in the beta, whatever else is decided: voluntary reports.** `lanes report` writes a local, redacted bundle (counts, versions, timings; no paths, titles, prompts, emails). The user reads it and sends it if and when they choose; we do not ask for it. Nothing is sent automatically. (#170)

**Step 2, only if accepted here: opt-in network telemetry.**
- **Default off.** A setting `lanes.telemetry` (default `off`) and a one-time, dismissible message at first run that explains it and defaults to "No". `LANES_TELEMETRY=0`, `DO_NOT_TRACK=1` and VS Code's own `telemetry.telemetryLevel: off` always win.
- **What is collected** (a closed list, in code; adding to it needs a new ADR):

| Field | Why | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Random **team id** generated by the server, and random **install id** per machine | Count weekly active teams without knowing who they are | UUID, not derived from the repository, user, email or machine name; can be reset |
| Lanes version, OS family (win, mac, linux), VS Code or fork name and version, Claude Code version | Compatibility matrix, R6 | Strings from a short allow-list |
| Daily counters: collisions handled, queue waits (bucketed: under 0.5 s, under 2 s, under 10 s, over), refused edits, lease TTL expiries, publish failures | The north-star and MVP metrics | Integers and bucket counts |
| Hook latency p50 and p95 | Latency target | Milliseconds, bucketed |
| Install-to-first-agent duration, once | Onboarding target | Seconds, bucketed |
| Team size (number of distinct people that day) | Weekly active teams, invites | A number, no names |

- **What is never collected:** file paths or names, repository names or URLs, branch names, commit ids, task titles, prompts, display names, emails, IP addresses (the endpoint does not log them), error text, anything free-form.
- **Transparency:** `Lanes: Show Telemetry Payload` shows the next batch; the outbox is a plain file in `~/.lanes/`; a batch is sent at most once a day over HTTPS to an endpoint on our own domain.
- **Retention:** raw batches 90 days, aggregates 13 months. Deleting by install id on request.
- **Cost:** a serverless function and key-value store on a free tier; about $0 at beta volume. Not on the critical path of the beta.

### How it fits the privacy policy
The policy changes **before** the release that ships Step 2 (it already promises this, privacy.html §10, and the upload guide §8 repeats it):
1. §2 "short version": "sends **nothing** to us" becomes "sends nothing to us unless you turn on anonymous usage statistics".
2. §3 table: a new row (data above, purpose: improve the product and measure our own beta, legal basis: **consent**, art. 6(1)(a), retention as above). Random ids are probably not personal data on their own; storing them on a device also touches the ePrivacy rules, so consent covers both. The lawyer confirms (#159).
3. The cookie policy stays correct: the website still sets no cookies; telemetry is not a website matter.
4. The terms and the beta invitation repeat it in one sentence.

## Alternatives considered
| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Opt-in, off by default, minimal (proposed)** | Honest with the promise; measures what the metrics need; repeatable for Stage 2 | 4 days to build; an endpoint to run; policy and consent work; most users will leave it off, so the sample is biased towards friendly teams |
| **B: None; only voluntary reports** | Zero infrastructure and zero legal surface; strongest privacy story; enough for 10 teams | Metrics depend on teams remembering to send; not enough for Stage 2; "weekly active teams" stays self-reported |
| C: Opt-out | Larger sample | Breaks the policy and the product promise; consent problems; rejected |
| D: Heartbeat only (a ping per server per day) | Smaller than A | Still needs the endpoint, consent and policy text; gives only "active", so little more than B |

## Consequences
- **Positive:** the beta can start without any of this (Step 1 is enough); the decision is made with data from the beta about whether voluntary reports were enough.
- **Negative / trade-offs:** if B is chosen, the success metrics table must say that the heartbeat and onboarding telemetry sources are replaced by the reports teams choose to send.
- **Follow-ups:** if A is accepted, build #171, update the policy first, add a test that a fresh install sends nothing, and update the success metrics sources. If B is accepted, edit the success metrics sources and mark #171 as won't do.
- **Revisit trigger:** before Stage 2, or if fewer than 6 teams send a `lanes report` summary or written feedback in weeks 2 to 4 of the beta.
